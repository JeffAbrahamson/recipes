\dish{鱼香茄子}
\hiragana{yú xiāng qiézi}
\altdish{Sichuan garlic eggplant}
\altdish{Eggplant, Sichuan garlic}
\serves{3--4}
\prep{15 minutes salting, 20--30 minutes frying, 5 minutes in the wok}
\source{openai-chatgpt}
\label{yuxiang-qiezi}

\begin{ingredients}
  \ingr{2}{}{Chinese eggplants (long, thin)}
  \ingr{}{}{salt}
  \ingrS{}{}{neutral oil for frying (2--3~cm in a Dutch oven)}
  \ingr{1}{\tbsp}{light soy sauce}
  \ingr{1}{\tbsp}{Chinkiang black vinegar}
  \ingr{1--2}{tsp}{sugar}
  \ingr{2--3}{\tbsp}{water or stock}
  \ingrS{1}{tsp}{cornstarch (optional)}
  \ingr{\fracH--1}{tsp}{green Sichuan peppercorns, lightly crushed}
  \ingr{1}{\tbsp}{doubanjiang (Pixian if possible)}
  \ingr{50--80}{g}{minced pork (optional)}
  \ingr{4--6}{}{garlic cloves, finely chopped}
  \ingr{1}{\tbsp}{ginger, finely chopped}
  \ingrS{2}{}{scallions, whites and greens separated}
  \ingr{}{}{chili oil (optional)}
\end{ingredients}


\begin{recipe}
  \begin{enumerate}

  \item Cut eggplant into batons 5--7~cm long, no thicker than a
    thumb.  Salt lightly, rest 10--15~minutes, pat dry.

  \item Heat oil to \CF{170--180}{340--355}.  Fry eggplant in small
    batches, keeping the oil bubbling actively, until slumped,
    flexible, slightly translucent and creamy when squeezed
    (3--5~minutes, longer if the oil cools).  Drain.

  \item Mix soy sauce, vinegar, sugar, water and cornstarch.

  \item Strain the frying oil into a jar, keeping 1--2~\tbsp{} for the
    wok.

  \item Medium-high heat.  Fry peppercorns until fragrant
    (5--10~seconds).  Add doubanjiang and fry until the oil turns red
    (20--40~seconds).  Add pork, if using, and cook through.  Add
    garlic, ginger and scallion whites; fry until fragrant without
    browning the garlic (20--60~seconds).

  \item Add the sauce, then the eggplant.  Toss to coat and reduce
    1--3~minutes, until almost no free liquid remains.

  \item Finish with scallion greens, chili oil, and optionally a
    splash of vinegar and a pinch of freshly ground peppercorns.

  \end{enumerate}
\end{recipe}

\notes{} The eggplant must be fully cooked before saucing, or it soaks
up the sauce and turns greasy.  Doneness comes late and suddenly:
spongy, then oil-logged, then abruptly silky.  If it is slow, cut
smaller or fry smaller batches.

The sauce is a glaze, not a gravy: a piece lifted with chopsticks
should not drip.  Err towards less liquid; a spoonful of water can be
added at the end.

The strained frying oil keeps 2--4~weeks in a dark jar (3--6~uses);
discard if sticky or rancid.  It will carry a faint Sichuan aroma.
